Simulation and Optical Design of Multilayer Transparent Conducting Oxides

NCPRE · IIT BombayThin-film opticsIZO / Ag / IZO

Abstract

A fabrication-aware study of IZO/Ag/IZO transparent electrodes for photovoltaic applications, using simulation as a design limit rather than a guarantee of fabricated-device performance.

The thesis used SETFOS and Python to explore how layer thickness, ultrathin-silver optical behaviour and electrical constraints interact. The project began in photovoltaics but became increasingly focused on thin-film interference, nanoscale optical constants and light–matter interaction.

Research question

Transparency, conductivity & light

Can an ultrathin metal layer reduce electrical loss while the surrounding oxide layers are tuned to preserve or improve optical transmission?

Methodology

01

Material inputs

Prepared and compared wavelength-dependent optical-constant datasets for IZO and silver, including corrected treatment of ultrathin Ag rather than relying only on bulk values.

02

SETFOS sweeps

Systematically varied top IZO, silver and bottom IZO thicknesses to identify promising optical stacks.

03

Python analysis

Calculated transmission, reflection, absorption, photon flux, theoretical current caps and comparative performance metrics.

04

Physical interpretation

Connected the simulated response to Volmer–Weber island growth, film continuity, wetting-layer effects and localized surface plasmon resonance considerations.

Experimental realism

Why fabricated films differ

Continuity

Sub-10 nm silver can form islands before becoming a continuous conducting film.

Morphology

Roughness, grains and thickness non-uniformity introduce scattering and broaden optical response.

Interfaces

Interfacial diffusion and imperfect boundaries change both electrical and optical behaviour.

Measured constants

Real deposited films may differ from the optical constants assumed in simulation.
