High-pressure Raman spectroscopy of Na₂Cu₂TeO₆

HP&SRPD · BARCDiamond anvil cellRaman spectroscopy

Abstract

A summer project in the High Pressure & Synchrotron Radiation Physics Division at BARC, supervised by Dr. Subodha Sahoo.

Na₂Cu₂TeO₆ was studied under pressure to examine the evolution of its vibrational modes and identify spectral changes that could motivate further structural investigation.

Methodology

01

Cell preparation

A stainless-steel gasket was pre-indented and a central hole was prepared as the DAC sample chamber before cleaning and mounting.

02

Pressure environment

The sample was loaded with ruby as an in-situ pressure calibrant and a 4:1 methanol–ethanol mixture to provide quasi-hydrostatic conditions.

03

Optical alignment

The DAC and Raman optical path were aligned carefully before collecting spectra at successive pressure points using a 532 nm laser.

04

Analysis

Pressure-dependent Raman measurements extended to approximately 12 GPa and spectra were analysed using Origin.

Interpretation

Pressure evolution of phonon modes

Redistribution of intensity between low-frequency peaks and merging/new features near 7 GPa were observed. These were treated as indications requiring further HP-XRD confirmation, not as a definitive phase-transition claim.
